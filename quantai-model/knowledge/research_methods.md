# QuantAI local knowledge: research methods

Source basis: established general research-methods principles. This file is a concise educational reference, not a substitute for a field-specific protocol or supervisor review.

## Question and design alignment
Use the research question to define the population, exposure/intervention, comparator, outcome, setting and time frame. Descriptive questions estimate distributions; analytic questions examine associations; causal questions require explicit assumptions and a design that addresses confounding. Cross-sectional studies measure exposure and outcome at roughly the same time and usually cannot establish temporal order. Cohort studies follow participants over time. Case-control studies select participants by outcome status and compare prior exposures. Randomised trials allocate interventions using a random process and need appropriate allocation concealment, follow-up and analysis.

## Sampling and generalisation
Probability sampling gives eligible units a known non-zero chance of selection and supports design-based inference when correctly implemented. Convenience or purposive sampling can be appropriate for exploratory or qualitative aims but limits statistical generalisation. Sample size should reflect the primary outcome, target precision or effect, significance level, power, design, expected non-response and planned subgroup analyses. Cluster sampling and complex survey designs often require design effects and survey-aware variance estimation.

## Confounding and bias
Confounding occurs when a third factor is associated with both exposure and outcome and is not a mediator on the causal pathway. Select adjustment variables using subject knowledge and a causal diagram when possible, not solely by univariable p-values. Selection bias arises from who enters or remains in a study. Information bias arises from measurement or classification errors. Reverse causation is plausible when outcome status can influence exposure. Statistical adjustment cannot automatically remove unmeasured confounding or repair poor measurement.

## Missing data
Report the amount and pattern of missingness by important variables. Missing completely at random means missingness is independent of observed and unobserved data; missing at random means it can be explained by observed data; missing not at random depends on unobserved values even after conditioning on observed data. Complete-case analysis can lose precision and may be biased. Multiple imputation may be appropriate under defensible assumptions, with sensitivity analysis when missing-not-at-random mechanisms are plausible. Never assume numeric codes such as 99 or 999 mean missing without checking the codebook.

## Interpretation
A p-value measures how incompatible the observed data (or more extreme data) are with a specified null model and its assumptions. It is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true. Report effect sizes and confidence intervals, not p-values alone. A confidence interval communicates uncertainty under the model and sampling assumptions; it does not account for every source of bias. Statistical significance does not establish clinical, public-health or practical importance. Observational association alone does not establish causation.

## Reporting
Use the reporting guideline appropriate to the design: STROBE for observational studies, CONSORT for randomised trials, PRISMA for systematic reviews, STARD for diagnostic accuracy, TRIPOD for prediction models, and COREQ or SRQR for qualitative research. Check the official guideline and journal requirements before submission.
